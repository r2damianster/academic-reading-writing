Classification

A classification divides a concept into categories and answers the reader’s question: <em>what types are there?</em> Its signature is a verb that announces the pattern and an enumeration that makes the limits of each category visible.

The model

This sentence comes from an open-access article about translation and interpreting training. Find the verb that announces the pattern.

The International Association of Conference Interpreters (AIIC) classifies interpreters’ working languages as A (native/active), B (non-native but near-native, also active), and C (passive, used for comprehension only).
Lozano-Argüelles, C., & Martínez-Gómez, A. (2025). Language learning bridge: How translation and interpreting training impacts bilingual proficiency in heritage and L2 learners. Frontiers in Education, 10, Article 1609736. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2025.1609736

Taking it apart

Label each segment: <strong>Classifier</strong> (announces the pattern) or <strong>Category</strong>.

The International Association of Conference Interpreters (AIIC) classifies interpreters’ working languages as
A (native/active),
B (non-native but near-native, also active),
and C (passive, used for comprehension only).
«La enumeración hace visibles los límites de cada categoría; cada letra recibe su propia definición entre paréntesis.»

Is it a classification?

Which sentence is a <strong>classification</strong> and not a cause-effect or process sentence?

A. Sediment contamination rose because wastewater was discharged into the estuary.
B. Heavy metals can be classified into three groups according to their toxicity.
C. Samples were first collected, then filtered and finally analysed.
Respuesta: B («can be classified into…»). A es causa-efecto («because»); C es proceso («first… then… finally»).
