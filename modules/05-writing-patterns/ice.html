ICE: The Quotation Sandwich

Citing a source is not pasting a phrase between quotation marks. Every well-integrated quotation does three things, in sequence: it is Introduced, it is Cited and it is Explained. Its absence produces the most visible defect of student writing: the orphan quotation, which appears without warning and disappears without comment.

Pattern: ICE (Introduce, Cite, Explain) · About 20 minutes · Book chapter 15

The model

This fragment comes from the discussion of the 2015 gender-gap study. It contains a complete ICE sandwich.

There are good reasons for such an interest, because as Cohen and Dörnyei (2002, p. 172) put it, “Motivation is often seen as the key learner variable because without it, nothing much happens.” Several studies have observed that women are more motivated than men in L2 learning.
Van der Slik, F. W. P., Van Hout, R. W. N. M., & Schepens, J. J. (2015). The gender gap in second language acquisition: Gender differences in the acquisition of Dutch among immigrants from 88 countries with 49 mother tongues. PLoS ONE, 10(11), e0142056. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0142056

Why this works

A quotation arrives without an Introduce and without an Explain. What is the reader’s main problem?

The reader does not know why they are reading the quotation, nor what it means for the argument.
The quotation is too short to be useful.
The author’s name appears twice.
Respuesta: A. «Una cita sin Introduce llega sin aviso… Una cita sin Explain se queda flotando.» El verbo de reporte del Introduce ya comunica la postura (Cap. 9).

Explain, don’t repeat

The quotation is: “Motivation is often seen as the key learner variable because without it, nothing much happens.” Which sentence is a good Explain?

In other words, motivation is the key variable because nothing happens without it.
This statement suggests that motivation may be the mediating variable between gender and L2 performance, which would help account for the gap.
Cohen and Dörnyei wrote this in 2002.
Respuesta: B. A repite la cita con otras palabras; C solo añade dato bibliográfico. El Explain debe aportar interpretación nueva (Apéndice C: «la motivación actúa como variable mediadora entre el género y el desempeño en L2»).

Rebuild it: rescue the orphan quotation

“Motivation is often seen as the key learner variable because without it, nothing much happens.” Female learners in this study also outperformed male learners in speaking and writing.

This quotation is an orphan: it has no Introduce and no Explain. Rewrite the fragment adding the two missing moves. Use Cohen and Dörnyei (2002, p. 172) as the source.

The Introduce names the source with author, year and page (e.g. “As Cohen and Dörnyei (2002, p. 172) explain,”) and uses a reporting verb before the verbatim quotation.
The Explain connects the quotation with the paragraph’s argument (for example, motivation as a mediating variable in the gender gap) and does not just repeat the quotation.
Modelo (Apéndice C): Introduce: «As Cohen and Dörnyei (2002, p. 172) explain,» y un Explain que conecte la cita con el argumento propio.

Spot it in the wild

Find an article from your field in open access and locate a direct quotation. Identify the three moments.

  1. Does the Introduce give the author’s name and some context, or does it only say “he said”?
  2. Does the Explain repeat the quotation in other words, or does it add a new interpretation?

Not graded. Bring your answers to class and compare them with a classmate.

Now you write

You will write your own paragraph using the ICE (Introduce, Cite, Explain) pattern. Allow about 12 minutes. Your writing is tracked for academic integrity: write it yourself, in your own words.

Find a real quotation from an article in your field and write in English a short paragraph that integrates it with the complete ICE scheme.
