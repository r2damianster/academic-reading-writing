Process Description

A process description orders a sequence of steps and answers the reader’s question: how, and in what order? It is the pattern that most often works without visible signals: real methods rely on the order of the verbs, not on first, then and finally.

The model

This sentence pair comes from the methods section of the spaced-retrieval study. Look for any “first”, “then” or “finally”.

Participants completed a pretest and posttest, during which their oral responses were recorded and analyzed using PRAAT software to measure MLR and AR. Target Vocabulary Usage was also calculated through percentage comparisons.
Faytong-Haro, M. (2025). Unlocking words and fluency: Spaced Retrieval small-scale case study practice with Spanish-speaking A1 EFL adult learners. Frontiers in Education, 10, Article 1715111. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2025.1715111

Taking it apart

Even without first/then, the order is clear. Put each segment in its step.

Participants completed a pretest and posttest,
during which their oral responses were recorded
and analyzed using PRAAT software to measure MLR and AR.
Target Vocabulary Usage was also calculated through percentage comparisons.
«No hay marcadores explícitos como first o then. La secuencia se sostiene con verbos encadenados… y con la conjunción “during which”.»

Why this works

Real methods rarely mark each step with first, then, finally; they trust the reader to follow the chain of verbs.

When your own process is confusing, the solution is not always adding connectors: sometimes it is enough to order the verbs better.

Moraleja del Cap. 12: no depender solo de los señalizadores para reconocer ni para escribir un proceso.

Rebuild it: order the steps

These four sentences describe a data-collection method, but they are out of order. Drag them into the sequence in which the steps happened.

Participants were first asked to complete a background questionnaire.
They then completed a timed writing task under controlled conditions.
Their responses were subsequently recorded for later analysis.
Finally, the recordings were transcribed and coded by two independent raters.
Orden (libro): (b), (c), (d), (a). Verificar que cada paso dependa lógicamente del anterior.

How is the order carried?

A methods paragraph contains no “first”, “then” or “finally”, yet the order of the steps is clear. What carries the sequence?

The chain of verbs and linking structures such as “during which”.
The citations in parentheses.
The tense of the first sentence only.

Phrase bank: process

Save the phrases you want to reuse. They go to your personal notebook.

First, X was done. Then, Y followed. The process began with X and concluded with Y. Participants completed [task], during which [measure] was recorded.

Now you write

You will write your own paragraph using the Process description pattern. Allow about 10 minutes. Your writing is tracked for academic integrity: write it yourself, in your own words.

Write in English a short process paragraph of three to five sentences describing how something is done in your field (a method, a procedure, a routine). Make the order of the steps unambiguous.

Self-assessment rubric: Process description

Before you submit, check your paragraph against each row. You can ask the AI for a quick check (maximum two times): it suggests the marks and you decide. If three or more rows need work, rewrite before you hand it in.

This pattern
I chose one pattern; I did not mix process with cause and effect or classification.
I use at least one signal or verb sequence that marks the order of the steps.
The order of the steps is unambiguous, even if I do not use first/then in every sentence.
A reader who reads only my first sentence can anticipate that a process follows.
General writing criteria
Academic register: formal tone, no contractions or colloquial expressions.
Vocabulary: precise, specific words instead of vague ones (thing, stuff, a lot of).
Cohesion: connectors and reference words link each sentence to the previous one.
Grammar and mechanics: complete sentences, agreement, punctuation, spelling.
Focus: the whole paragraph develops one main idea.
