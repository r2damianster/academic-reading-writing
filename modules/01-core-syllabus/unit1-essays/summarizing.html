Summarizing in 3 Moves

A summary is much shorter than the original. It keeps only the central idea and the main arguments, in your own words, and it always names the author.

1. Find the central idea (what is the whole text about?)
2. Keep the main arguments (the reasons that support it)
3. Cut examples, statistics, and small details — then cite the author

In this lesson you will practise by building summaries one piece at a time.

Enfoque: lección práctica; la teoría es mínima a propósito. Un resumen es 20–40 % del original. Diferencia clave con la paráfrasis (lección siguiente): el resumen reduce, la paráfrasis reformula con longitud similar.

Model: Original vs. Summary

"Writing an effective academic summary requires reading comprehension and text reduction skills. Novice writers frequently struggle because they copy sentences almost directly from the source without recognizing rhetorical organization. Consequently, their summaries often lack coherence and sound fragmented, relying on superficial lexical substitution."
Synthesizing academic sources requires structural comprehension over simple text substitution (Swales & Feak, 2012).
Qué observar: el resumen conserva solo la tesis (resumir exige comprender la estructura, no sustituir palabras), elimina la descripción de los escritores novatos y mantiene autor y año.

Build It #1: Sleep and Learning

Practice text (written for this course):

Many university students treat sleep as a flexible part of their schedule. When exams approach, they reduce their nightly rest to make room for extra study hours, convinced that more time with the material will lead to better results.

Research on memory suggests the opposite. During sleep, the brain moves new information from short-term to long-term storage, a process that cannot be completed if sleep is cut short. Students who sleep fewer than six hours also show lower attention the next day, so the hours they gain for study are used less effectively.

For example, in one experiment, students who slept eight hours remembered 20% more vocabulary than classmates who stayed awake to review. Therefore, protecting sleep is not a distraction from studying but a part of it.

Step 1 — Central idea. Write ONE sentence with the central idea of the whole text.

The text argues that
Sleeping enough is part of effective studying / cutting sleep to study more is counterproductive.
Expresa la idea central del texto (dormir lo suficiente es parte del estudio efectivo; sacrificar el sueño para estudiar es contraproducente) en una sola oración, con sus propias palabras y sin incluir el ejemplo del experimento.

Step 2 — First argument. Add ONE sentence with the first main argument (why does sleep matter for memory?).

First,
During sleep the brain transfers information from short-term to long-term memory; cutting sleep prevents this.
Agrega, en una oración, el primer argumento principal: durante el sueño el cerebro consolida la información (de memoria de corto a largo plazo) y recortar el sueño lo impide. No incluye el dato del 20 %.

Step 3 — Second argument. Add ONE sentence with the second main argument (what happens to attention?). Your 3 sentences together are your summary.

Second,
Sleeping fewer than six hours lowers next-day attention, so extra study time is less effective.
The three sentences together give the central idea plus both arguments, without the experiment example.
Incluye el segundo argumento: dormir poco reduce la atención al día siguiente, por lo que las horas extra de estudio rinden menos.
El resumen completo (3 oraciones) conserva la idea central y ambos argumentos, elimina ejemplos y cifras, y no copia frases literales del texto.

Build It #2: Green Spaces

Practice text (fictional source written for this course): Lozano (2019)

Cities are growing quickly, and many residents now live far from nature. Lozano (2019) argues that urban parks and gardens should be treated as public health infrastructure, not as decoration.

First, contact with green areas lowers stress. In her survey, people who spent thirty minutes in a park reported feeling calmer than those who walked along a busy street. Second, parks encourage physical activity, because they give residents safe and free places to walk, run, or play sports.

The author notes that a small neighbourhood park in her study attracted about 300 visitors each week. She concludes that city planners should invest in green space just as they invest in hospitals.

Step 1 — Central idea + author. One sentence with the central idea. Name the author and year: Lozano (2019) or (Lozano, 2019).

Urban green spaces should be considered public health infrastructure / an investment for city health, not decoration.
Expresa la idea central (los espacios verdes urbanos deben tratarse como infraestructura de salud pública) en una oración con sus propias palabras.
Atribuye la idea al autor con nombre y año (Lozano, 2019) de forma correcta.

Step 2 — Two arguments. Add ONE sentence that includes BOTH main arguments (there are two: one about stress and one about physical activity).

Green areas reduce stress.
Parks encourage physical activity.
Menciona el primer argumento: el contacto con áreas verdes reduce el estrés.
Menciona el segundo argumento: los parques fomentan la actividad física.

Step 3 — Author's conclusion. Add ONE sentence with what the author concludes. Start it with a link word (Therefore, / As a result, / Consequently,).

Planners should invest in green space like they invest in hospitals.
Total summary omits the survey details and the 300-visitors figure.
Incluye la conclusión de la autora: los planificadores urbanos deben invertir en áreas verdes tal como invierten en hospitales.
El resumen completo es coherente, usa un conector y omite detalles (encuesta, cifra de 300 visitantes) sin copiar frases literales.

Build It #3: Social Media and Attention

Practice text (fictional source written for this course): Peña (2022)

Peña (2022) examines how social media platforms shape the way young adults read. Most platforms are designed to deliver short pieces of content in rapid succession, and users quickly learn to scroll instead of reading closely.

According to the author, this habit has two consequences. Readers become less patient with long, demanding texts, and they retain less of what they read because their attention shifts every few seconds. In one of her interviews, a student admitted that she had not finished a full article in months.

Peña does not call for abandoning social media. Instead, she recommends that universities teach students to read deliberately, for example by setting fixed periods of uninterrupted reading.

Step 1. Write the central idea in ONE sentence, with the author and year.

Social media platforms change/weaken the way young adults read (habit of scrolling instead of reading closely).
Expresa la idea central: las redes sociales moldean/debilitan la forma en que los jóvenes leen (hábito de deslizar en lugar de leer con atención).
Cita correctamente a la autora con nombre y año (Peña, 2022).

Step 2. Add the main arguments (the two consequences) in one or two sentences.

Readers become less patient with long, demanding texts.
Readers retain less because their attention shifts constantly.
Incluye la primera consecuencia: menos paciencia con textos largos y exigentes.
Incluye la segunda consecuencia: se retiene menos porque la atención cambia constantemente.

Step 3. Finish with the author's recommendation. Your whole paragraph must be at most about one third of the original length.

Author recommends teaching students to read deliberately (e.g. periods of uninterrupted reading), not abandoning social media.
Incluye la recomendación de la autora: enseñar a los estudiantes a leer de forma deliberada (p. ej. períodos de lectura ininterrumpida), sin abandonar las redes sociales.
El párrafo completo es un resumen coherente y fiel: idea central, dos consecuencias y recomendación, sin la anécdota de la entrevista y sin copiar frases literales.

Writing Task Instructions

Now you will write a full summary on your own. It should take about 5 minutes. Use everything you practised: central idea, main arguments, no examples, and cite the author.

Read the text and write one paragraph of 60–90 words that summarizes it. Include the central idea, the two main problems, and cite the author (Ibarra, 2020). Do not copy sentences.

Practice text (fictional source written for this course): Ibarra (2020)
Ibarra (2020) studies why so many students abandon language courses after a few months. In many programmes, learners are taught grammar rules first and are only expected to speak much later. As a result, they feel unprepared when they finally have to hold a conversation, and their motivation drops.

The author identifies two main problems. First, classes rarely include speaking practice in the early weeks, so students never build confidence. Second, mistakes are usually corrected in front of the whole class, which makes learners afraid of speaking. One student in the study said she preferred silence to being corrected publicly.

Ibarra proposes starting speaking practice from the first week, in small groups, and giving feedback privately. In the pilot course where these changes were tested, dropout fell from 35% to 12%.
