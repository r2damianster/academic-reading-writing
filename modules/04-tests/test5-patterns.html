Test 6 — Writing Patterns

This assessment evaluates your ability to choose and apply the four paragraph patterns studied in the Writing Patterns module within one short, coherent text.

Topic: "Should every university degree in Ecuador require an English-medium course?"

This test has two phases:

  1. Phase 1 — Planning: choose the pattern for each of your four paragraphs and write its opening sentence.
  2. Phase 2 — Writing: develop the four paragraphs, applying the patterns you chose, in the same order.
PatternComponents
SPRESituation, Problem, Response, Evaluation
PEELPoint, Evidence, Explanation, Link
CERClaim, Evidence (with data), Reasoning
ToulminAt least four of Claim, Grounds, Warrant, Backing, Qualifier, Rebuttal (a Warrant is required)
  • Four paragraphs, four patterns: use each pattern exactly once. You decide which pattern goes in which paragraph and in what order.
  • Choose with a purpose: pick the pattern that best fits what that paragraph does for your answer to the topic. The four paragraphs must work together as one coherent text.
  • Opening sentence: in Phase 1 you write only the first sentence of each paragraph (the Situation for SPRE, the Point for PEEL, the Claim for CER and Toulmin).
  • No labels: do not write the names of the components (Point, Claim, Warrant...) in your paragraphs.
  • Data: in the CER paragraph you may invent a small, plausible data set (two groups, two means, a difference).
  • Hedging and nominalization: include at least one hedge and one nominalization in every paragraph.
  • Register: formal academic English, no contractions. Target: 350–500 words in total.
Note: Strikethroughs and erasures are allowed, as long as they do not interrupt the teacher's reading.
