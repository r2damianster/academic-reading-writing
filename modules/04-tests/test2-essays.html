Test 2 — Essays

This assessment evaluates your ability to build a complete argumentative essay from a given thesis, using the PEER paragraph model, organizational connectors, academic vocabulary, and nominalizations.

Given Thesis: "Despite the rapid proliferation of advanced translation software and artificial intelligence, specialized higher education in the English language remains indispensable for the development of cross-cultural mediation and nuanced global communication."

This test has two phases:

  1. Phase 1 — Planning: Draft 3 Topic Sentences, one for each argument.
  2. Phase 2 — Writing: Develop your 3 argument paragraphs plus a conclusion, applying PEER, organizational connectors, AWL vocabulary, and nominalizations.
  • Three Arguments: Develop exactly 3 arguments supporting the given thesis, each in its own paragraph.
  • PEER Model (Point, Evidence, Explanation, Relevance): Every argument paragraph must strictly follow Point → Evidence → Explanation → Relevance. Relevance is where you link the paragraph back to the thesis.
  • Heading Vocabulary: Use organizational connectors to signal structure and link ideas between and within paragraphs (avoid basic sequencers like firstly/secondly/finally; prefer sophisticated transitions).
  • Academic Vocabulary (AWL): Organically incorporate at least 2 distinct words from the Academic Word List studied in the Toolbox lessons.
  • Nominalizations: Convert verbs/adjectives into nouns (e.g., develop → development, integrate → integration) throughout the essay to raise formality and academic rigor.
  • Conclusion: Close with a conclusion paragraph that restates the thesis and synthesizes the three arguments — no new ideas.

AWL suggestion bank:
