The Academic Sentence: Given and New

Every well-built academic sentence handles two flows at once: it picks up something the reader already knows (given information) and delivers something new. The grammatical tool that makes this chain possible is the nominalization: turning a process into a noun so that what was new becomes the given of the next sentence.

Pattern: Given–New and light nominalization · About 20 minutes · Book chapter 2

Objetivo: contrato dado/nuevo (entre oraciones y dentro de una oración) + nominalización ligera. Modelo: Watson y Crick (1953), usado dos veces. Anticipa hedging (Cap. 16).

The model

In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick described the double-helix structure of DNA in Nature. We use their article twice. First, the two opening sentences; then the famous final sentence.

We wish to suggest a structure for the salt of deoxyribose nucleic acid (D.N.A.). This structure has novel features which are of considerable biological interest.
It has not escaped our notice that the specific pairing we have postulated immediately suggests a possible copying mechanism for the genetic material.
Watson, J. D., & Crick, F. H. C. (1953). Molecular structure of nucleic acids: A structure for deoxyribose nucleic acid. Nature, 171(4356), 737–738. https://doi.org/10.1038/171737a0
Fuente real. «structure» ya es una nominalización: Watson y Crick empaquetan su propuesta completa en una sola palabra.

Taking it apart (between sentences)

Look at the two opening sentences. Is each segment Given (the reader already has it) or New (introduced for the first time)?

We wish to suggest a structure for the salt of deoxyribose nucleic acid (D.N.A.).
This structure
has novel features which are of considerable biological interest.
Mecanismo: los autores empaquetan algo en un sustantivo («a structure»); la segunda oración lo retoma con «This structure» y avanza.

Taking it apart (inside one sentence)

The final sentence applies the same contract inside a single sentence. Label each segment: Given, Bridge (the verb that connects) or New.

the specific pairing we have postulated
immediately suggests
a possible copying mechanism for the genetic material.
Observación a retomar en el Cap. 16: la oración está envuelta en hedges deliberados («It has not escaped our notice», «suggests», «a possible»).

Why this works

The given–new contract imitates how readers process information: first they look for a familiar foothold, then they accept something new resting on it. When a text breaks the contract — for example, by starting every sentence with a different topic that picks up nothing from the previous one — the reader feels they are jumping around with no thread.

At sentence scale, nominalization is the grammatical resource that keeps the thread: X increases → This increase…; Y was analyzed → This analysis…. At paragraph scale, the same move (known anchor, new advance) is organised by patterns such as PEEL.

Spot the pattern: Darwin

Here is the last sentence of On the Origin of Species (Darwin, 1859): “There is grandeur in this view of life, with its several powers, having been originally breathed into a few forms or into one…” Within the book, what role does the phrase “this view of life” play?

Given information: the reader already knows the view of life developed in the book.
New information: it is introduced here for the first time.
Respuesta: A. «this» marca información ya conocida por el lector tras todo el libro. Fuente: Darwin (1859), dominio público.

Rebuild it: restore the chain

These three sentences come from a hypothetical scientific text. Put them in the order that makes each sentence pick up as given something the previous one presented as new.

Deforestation in tropical regions has accelerated significantly over the past decade.
The resulting habitat loss has led to a measurable reduction in local biodiversity.
This reduction in biodiversity has prompted conservation efforts across multiple countries.
Orden (libro, Apéndice C): (b), (c), (a). «Deforestation» introduce el proceso; «The resulting habitat loss» lo retoma; «This reduction» retoma la reducción que la anterior presentó como nueva. Nominalizaciones: deforestation, habitat loss, reduction, efforts.

Rebuild it: nominalize the process

Complete each second sentence with the noun that packages the process of the first sentence.

Prices increased sharply in 2023. This ___ affected low-income households.

The samples were analyzed in two laboratories. This ___ revealed no significant difference.

The solution reacted with the metal. This ___ produced a measurable amount of heat.

The research team observed the effect in both groups. This ___ supports the original hypothesis.

Rebuild it: extend the chain

(a) Deforestation in tropical regions has accelerated significantly over the past decade.
(b) The resulting habitat loss has led to a measurable reduction in local biodiversity.
(c) This reduction in biodiversity has prompted conservation efforts across multiple countries.

Write a fourth sentence that continues the chain. It must take as its given topic something that sentence (c) presented as new, and it should use a nominalization.

The fourth sentence starts from something new in sentence (c) (for example, “conservation efforts” or “these efforts”) and uses it as its given information.
The sentence adds genuinely new information and contains a nominalization (for example, “This expansion…”, “This protection…”) in academic English.
Ejemplos válidos: «These conservation efforts have so far focused on protected areas, with uneven results.» / «This protection has been uneven.» Lo esencial: tomar como tema algo nuevo de (c).

Spot it in the wild

Choose a paragraph from an open-access article in your field. Underline the nominalizations that link one sentence with the next.

  3. Is there any sentence where you had to go back to understand the link? What is missing?

Now you write

You will write your own paragraph using the Given–New and light nominalization pattern. Allow about 10 minutes. Your writing is tracked for academic integrity: write it yourself, in your own words.

Write a short paragraph in English, three to five sentences, about a process or phenomenon in your own field. Chain the sentences using the given–new contract, with at least one nominalization that turns a process into a noun.

General writing criteria
Academic register: formal tone, no contractions or colloquial expressions.
Vocabulary: precise, specific words instead of vague ones (thing, stuff, a lot of).
Cohesion: connectors and reference words link each sentence to the previous one.
Grammar and mechanics: complete sentences, agreement, punctuation, spelling.
Focus: the whole paragraph develops one main idea.
