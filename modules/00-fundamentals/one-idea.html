Introduction

What is it?

In academic writing, a paragraph is like a contract. When you start with a sentence, you promise the reader you will talk only about that specific topic.

The One-Point Rule means: One paragraph = One single idea.

Got it?

Does this opening sentence follow the One-Point Rule?
'Regular exercise improves heart health and also helps the local economy...'

A. Yes
B. No, it mixes Health and Economy
Respuesta: B.
Tip: Un párrafo debe tener un solo "hilo conductor". Si hablas de salud y economía en la misma oración inicial, el lector no sabrá cuál es el enfoque principal.

Quick Check

Your point is: 'Remote work increases productivity.' Which evidence fits?

A. Data: tasks finished 13% faster.
B. Survey: people missed office coffee.
Respuesta: A.
Tip: La evidencia debe sustentar la afirmación. Si dices que aumenta la productividad, un dato sobre café en la oficina es irrelevante para ese punto específico.

Why does it matter?

Facts don't speak for themselves; you have to explain them. Keep your analysis strictly focused on the same idea.

Practical Example

Point: Online learning offers flexibility for working students.
✔ Evidence: Students can access recorded lectures at any time of day.
✔ Closing: This adaptability makes higher education more accessible.

Apply It: Enforce the One-Point Rule

Paragraph draft (student submission):

"Studying abroad broadens a student's cultural perspective by exposing them to unfamiliar customs and daily routines. Living in another country also forces students to adapt quickly to unexpected situations, from navigating public transportation to resolving misunderstandings in a second language. Universities have recently increased tuition fees for international students, which has become a growing concern for many applicants."

One sentence in this paragraph breaks the One-Point Rule. Identify it and explain what unrelated topic it introduces. Then, write a replacement closing sentence that reinforces the paragraph's actual point instead of drifting to a new idea.

Identifica correctamente la tercera oración (sobre el aumento de tuition fees) como la violación del One-Point Rule, explicando que introduce un tema nuevo (costos/aranceles) desconectado del punto real del párrafo (adaptabilidad cultural).
Escribe una oración de cierre de reemplazo que refuerza el punto original (perspectiva cultural / capacidad de adaptación) conectando las dos evidencias ya dadas (costumbres y resolución de problemas), sin introducir un tema nuevo.
