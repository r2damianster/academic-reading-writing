Supporting Sentences

What Are Supporting Sentences?

Once your Topic Sentence makes a claim, the supporting sentences are the proof and development of that claim. There are five main types:

  • Example — a specific case that illustrates the point
  • Statistic — a number or data point from research
  • Quote — the exact words of an expert or study
  • Anecdote — a brief real-life story that shows the point in action
  • Definition — clarifying a key term used in the claim

A strong paragraph usually combines more than one type — but each sentence must connect to the same central idea.

• Definition: "...refers to the state of..." (aclaración de término).

The Chain Rule

Supporting sentences must form a chain, not a pile. Each sentence should connect logically to the one before it — not jump to a new idea.

Think of it as building a wall: every brick must sit on the previous one. If you skip a brick, the wall collapses.

Ask yourself after each sentence: "Does this follow naturally from what I just said?"

Find the Break

One sentence breaks the logical chain. Click on it.

Social media overuse has been linked to declining attention spans in teenagers. A 2023 study found that students who spent more than 4 hours daily on social platforms scored significantly lower on focus tests. Social media companies were originally founded in California and have since expanded globally. This loss of concentration affects not only academic performance but also classroom participation. Therefore, prolonged exposure to these platforms poses a measurable risk to cognitive development.
Respuesta: "Social media companies were originally founded in California..."
Tip: Esta oración rompe la cadena porque habla de la historia de las empresas, mientras que el resto del párrafo trata sobre los efectos cognitivos en los usuarios. Es un error de relevancia.

Linking Signals

Good supporting sentences use linking language to make the chain visible to the reader. Some of the most useful signals are:

  • For instance / For example — to introduce a specific case
  • Specifically — to narrow or zoom in on the point
  • This is evident in... — to connect evidence back to the claim
  • Furthermore / In addition — to add a new but related point
  • This suggests / This indicates — to begin your analysis of evidence

Notice: these signals tell the reader what kind of sentence is coming next before they even read it.

Complete the Chain

Regular physical activity strengthens the immune system. ___, a study published in the Journal of Sport Science found that people who exercised three times per week had 40% fewer sick days annually. ___ that even moderate exercise has a measurable protective effect on health.

Respuestas:
1. for instance: Introduce la evidencia (el estudio).
2. this suggests: Introduce el análisis de esa evidencia.
Tip: Sin estos conectores, las oraciones parecen datos aislados. Los conectores muestran la función de cada "ladrillo" en la pared.

Apply It: Extend the Chain

Paragraph draft (student submission):

"Regular reading has been shown to expand vocabulary in young learners. Reading regularly also helps children learn more words. Furthermore, this vocabulary growth is linked to stronger performance on standardized reading comprehension tests."

The second sentence just repeats the first one in different words instead of developing the point. Identify that sentence, then rewrite it as a genuinely new supporting sentence — a statistic, example, or quote — that adds real information and uses an appropriate linking signal (for instance, "For instance," or "According to...") to connect smoothly with the sentences around it.

Identifica correctamente la segunda oración ("Reading regularly also helps children learn more words") como una repetición de la primera sin información nueva, y la reemplaza con una oración de soporte genuinamente nueva (estadística, ejemplo o cita concreta) que desarrolla la afirmación en lugar de repetirla.
La oración de reemplazo usa una señal de enlace apropiada (p. ej. "For instance," o "According to...") que conecta con fluidez la oración temática (expansión de vocabulario) y la oración de cierre (rendimiento en pruebas de comprensión), manteniendo la cadena.
