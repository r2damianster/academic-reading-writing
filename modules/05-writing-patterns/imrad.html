IMRaD: The Macrostructure of a Paper

This lesson takes one more step: reading a whole empirical article by its macrostructure. IMRaD (Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion) is the skeleton of almost every quantitative paper. Each section has its own dominant verb tense and its own rhetorical function.

Pattern: IMRaD · About 20 minutes · Book chapter 14

Based on: Rodríguez Zambrano, A. D. (2026). Academic writing patterns: A practical guide for EFL university students [Unpublished manuscript]. Universidad Laica Eloy Alfaro de Manta.

The model

This is the same article you met in the CER lesson, now read from beginning to end. Four fragments, one per section.

Introduction. This small-scale case study explores the impact of Spaced Retrieval Practice (SRP) on vocabulary acquisition and oral fluency in Spanish-speaking A1 EFL adult learners, addressing the need for effective instructional strategies that enhance long-term retention and fluency development. Given the importance of lexical knowledge in oral proficiency, this research aims to determine the extent to which SRP influences Mean Length of Run (MLR), Articulation Rate (AR), and Target Vocabulary Usage.
Methods. A quasi-experimental design was implemented with eight adult learners enrolled in an A1-level English course. Participants completed a pretest and posttest, during which their oral responses were recorded and analyzed using PRAAT software to measure MLR and AR.
Results. The Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test comparing Target Vocabulary Usage between the pretest and posttest showed an increase in vocabulary integration, with the mean rising from 23.13% (SD = 9.57) to 27.52% (SD = 4.58). The median also increased from 21.08% to 27.37%. However, the difference was not statistically significant (W = 9, p = 0.117).
Discussion. Despite this, the effect size (r = 0.45) is considered medium.
Faytong-Haro, M. (2025). Unlocking words and fluency: Spaced Retrieval small-scale case study practice with Spanish-speaking A1 EFL adult learners. Frontiers in Education, 10, Article 1715111. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2025.1715111

Why IMRaD works

IMRaD answers the four questions an expert reader asks, in order:

  1. Introduction — why does this matter?
  2. Methods — what did they do?
  3. Results — what did they find?
  4. Discussion — what does it mean?

Each question needs the previous answer to make sense: you cannot evaluate a result without knowing the method that produced it, nor judge a method without knowing the question it tries to answer.

IMRaD also solves what to do with a non-significant finding: it reserves a fixed space, the last section, precisely for the interpretive work that a bare p-value cannot do.

Si un «Results» ya contiene una palabra evaluativa (surprisingly, notably, only), ¿por qué el autor adelantó la interpretación? Pregunta de discusión del libro.

Rebuild it: restore the abstract

These four sentences come from a real abstract, but they are out of order. Put them in IMRaD order.

Translation and interpreting training offers a promising context for advancing bilingual proficiency, integrating skill development while bridging second language acquisition and translation studies.
Twenty-three bilingual learners, primarily heritage Spanish speakers, enrolled in a semester-long translation and interpreting program and completed ACTFL Oral Proficiency Interviews at the beginning and end of the semester.
Results showed no significant overall gains in Spanish oral proficiency, but students with lower initial proficiency levels demonstrated greater gains across the semester.
This suggests that initial proficiency is a key factor in how much learners benefit from this type of instruction.
Orden (libro): (b), (a), (c), (d): Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion. (c) y (d) casi se fusionan: el dato y su interpretación aparecen en oraciones consecutivas. Fuente: Lozano-Argüelles y Martínez-Gómez (2025).

Spot it in the wild

Take an empirical article from your field in open access. Without reading the section headings, identify where the Introduction ends and Methods begins, and where Results ends and Discussion begins, guided only by tense and type of language.

  1. At which exact sentence does the text stop talking in the present about the general problem and start describing in the past what the authors did?
  2. Is there any sentence in Results that already contains an evaluative word (surprisingly, notably, only) before the Discussion? Why do you think the author brought the interpretation forward?

Not graded. Bring your answers to class and compare them with a classmate.

Now you write

You will write your own paragraph using the IMRaD pattern. Allow about 12 minutes. Your writing is tracked for academic integrity: write it yourself, in your own words.

Choose a real article from your field (or the data of your own work) and write four sentences in English, one per IMRaD section, that summarise that work. Do not write the section names.

Self-assessment rubric: IMRaD

Before you submit, check your paragraph against each row. You can ask the AI for a quick check (maximum two times): it suggests the marks and you decide. If three or more rows need work, rewrite before you hand it in.

CriterionAchievedNeeds work
This pattern
My Introduction sentence uses the present tense and explains why the topic matters.
My Methods sentence uses the past tense and describes an action, not a finding.
My Results sentence reports a concrete datum without interpreting it yet.
My Discussion sentence interprets that datum and does not simply repeat it.
General writing criteria
Academic register: formal tone, no contractions or colloquial expressions.
Vocabulary: precise, specific words instead of vague ones (thing, stuff, a lot of).
Cohesion: connectors and reference words link each sentence to the previous one.
Grammar and mechanics: complete sentences, agreement, punctuation, spelling.
Focus: the whole paragraph develops one main idea.
Error frecuente: interpretar ya en Results o repetir el dato en Discussion.
