Metadiscourse: Guiding the Reader

An academic text does not only inform: it also manages the reader’s attention. Metadiscourse is the set of resources that do that work: <strong>connectors</strong> that mark the logic between sentences, <strong>hedges</strong> that soften a claim and <strong>boosters</strong> that reinforce it. Choosing between a hedge and a booster is an epistemic decision about how much certainty the evidence allows.

Ojo: las dos primeras provienen de Tribushinina y Mackaaij; la tercera y cuarta de Van der Slik et al. (2015), citado en el libro como contraste «los hedges y los boosters son las dos caras de la misma herramienta».

Why this works

Metadiscourse works because it calibrates the certainty of a claim to the real level of the evidence. A text without hedges sounds more sure than the evidence allows; a text with too many hedges sounds unsure even when the evidence is solid. The expert reader detects the mismatch and discounts the author’s credibility.

Boosters do the opposite job. When the datum is genuinely robust, an explicit booster (remarkably robust and constant) tells the reader there is no need to qualify. Choosing between hedge and booster is not a stylistic gesture: it is a decision about how much certainty the data allow.

Hedges y boosters = dos caras de la misma herramienta.

Rebuild it: too categorical

“Bilingual education always improves academic outcomes for minority language students. Schools that do not offer bilingual programs are failing their students.”

This paragraph is <strong>too categorical</strong>. Rewrite it adding at least one hedge that acknowledges variation or exceptions.

The rewrite replaces absolute words (always, are failing) with calibrated language (tends to, often, may) and acknowledges variation or exceptions.
The paragraph keeps its message and is written in fluent academic English, without excessive hedging.
Modelo (Apéndice C): usar «tends to improve» en lugar de «always improves», y frases que admitan excepciones.

Rebuild it: too tentative

“It might perhaps be possible that extensive reading could maybe have some kind of effect on vocabulary, although this is not entirely certain and more research might be needed.”

This paragraph is <strong>over-hedged</strong>. Rewrite it, removing the overload of softeners and keeping only those needed for the real level of certainty.

The rewrite eliminates redundant hedges (might / perhaps / possible / could / maybe) and keeps at most one or two that reflect the real level of certainty.
The sentence is clear and direct, keeps the meaning (extensive reading and vocabulary, with further research needed) and sounds academic.
Modelo: «Extensive reading may have a positive effect on vocabulary, although further research is needed.» Un solo hedge que refleje el nivel real de certeza.
