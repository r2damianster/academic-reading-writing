Test 1 — PEEL Essentials

This assessment checks the essential pattern of the course: the PEEL paragraph. You will answer a question by building four PEEL paragraphs, each defending a different reason.

Question: "Should university students in Ecuador be allowed to use their phones during class?"

This test has two phases:

  1. Phase 1 — Planning: state your position and draft the Point of each of your four paragraphs.
  2. Phase 2 — Writing: develop four PEEL paragraphs that follow your plan.
  • Your position: answer the question with a clear position (yes, no, or yes under conditions). All four paragraphs must support it.
  • Four paragraphs, four reasons: each paragraph defends one reason, and the four reasons must be different from one another.
  • PEEL Model (Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link): every paragraph must follow Point → Evidence → Explanation → Link. The Evidence must be concrete (a datum, a source or a specific example). The Explanation must interpret the Evidence, not repeat it. The Link ties the paragraph back to the question or leads into the next paragraph.
  • One Point per paragraph: do not mix two ideas in the same paragraph.
  • No labels: do not write the names of the components (Point, Evidence...) in your paragraphs.
  • Register: formal academic English, no contractions, no vague words such as thing, stuff or a lot. No introduction or conclusion is needed.
  • Length: 300–450 words in total.
Note: Strikethroughs and erasures are allowed, as long as they do not interrupt the teacher's reading.
