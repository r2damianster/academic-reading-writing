The PEEL Family: MEAL, TEEL, PIE, TRI, WEED

Open ten writing manuals and you will find ten acronyms for the same structure: MEAL, TEEL, PIE, TRI, WEED. The labels change; the wave does not. Whoever masters one, masters them all. You will relabel the same paragraph with two systems and then drop a move on purpose.

Pattern: PEEL family · About 20 minutes · Book chapter 5

  1. The model — read a real, published passage
  2. Taking it apart — label each move
  3. Why this works — see the semantic wave
  4. Spot the pattern — recognise it in a second text
  5. Rebuild it — order, complete or repair a text
  6. Now you write — your own paragraph, with a checklist
  7. Phrase bank — keep the phrases you will reuse
Objetivo: equivalencia entre sistemas y variantes de 3 moves (PIE, TRI). Se reutiliza el modelo de PEEL (Van der Slik, 2015) para que el estudiante vea que cambian las etiquetas, no la estructura.

The model (again)

This is the same paragraph you met in the PEEL lesson. We will label it with two systems at once.

Female learners consistently outperformed male learners in speaking and writing proficiency in Dutch as a second language. This gender gap remained remarkably robust and constant when other learner characteristics were taken into account, such as education, age of arrival, length of residence and hours studying Dutch. For reading and listening skills in Dutch, no gender gap was found. … These findings do not seem to match nurture-oriented explanatory frameworks based for instance on a human capital approach or gender-specific acculturation processes. Rather, they seem to corroborate a nature-based, gene-environment correlational framework in which language proficiency being a genetically influenced ability interacting with environmental factors such as motivation, orientation, education, and learner strategies that still mediate between endowment and acquiring language proficiency at an adult stage.
Van der Slik, F. W. P., Van Hout, R. W. N. M., & Schepens, J. J. (2015). The gender gap in second language acquisition: Gender differences in the acquisition of Dutch among immigrants from 88 countries with 49 mother tongues. PLoS ONE, 10(11), e0142056. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0142056

Taking it apart: the MEAL system

You already know this paragraph as PEEL. Now label it with <strong>MEAL</strong>: Main idea, Evidence, Analysis, Link.

Female learners consistently outperformed male learners in speaking and writing proficiency in Dutch as a second language.
This gender gap remained remarkably robust and constant when other learner characteristics were taken into account… For reading and listening skills in Dutch, no gender gap was found.
These findings do not seem to match nurture-oriented explanatory frameworks…
Rather, they seem to corroborate a nature-based, gene-environment correlational framework…
Conclusión del libro: «Las etiquetas cambian; la estructura no.» Point = Main idea; Explanation = Analysis.

PIE and TRI have three moves instead of four. PIE drops the Link: the paragraph ends after the interpretation. TRI, proposed by Becker in 1965, works on levels of generality: Topic generalises, Restriction narrows it, Illustration makes it concrete. Despite these differences, the three core moves (idea, data, analysis) appear in every variant.

Becker (1965) describió los niveles de generalidad mucho antes de que PEEL se popularizara en la educación secundaria australiana (años 90). MEAL: writing centers de EE. UU.; WEED: mnemotecnia británica para estudiantes de quince años.

Spot the pattern: relabel a TEEL paragraph

This paragraph is built as TEEL (Topic sentence, Evidence, Explanation, Link). Relabel each sentence with the <strong>MEAL</strong> system.

Extensive reading programs have been shown to improve vocabulary acquisition more effectively than traditional instruction.
Nakanishi (2015) conducted a meta-analysis of 34 studies and reported a mean effect size of d = 0.71 in favor of extensive reading over conventional methods.
This effect is likely attributable to the high volume of contextualized input that extensive reading provides, which allows learners to encounter target words repeatedly in meaningful contexts.
These findings lend support to the inclusion of sustained silent reading components in EFL curricula at the university level.
Párrafo del libro (etiquetado TEEL). Fuente temática: Nakanishi (2015).

Rebuild it: the three-move PIE

Remove the Link. Put these three sentences into PIE order: Point, Illustration, Explanation.

Extensive reading programs have been shown to improve vocabulary acquisition more effectively than traditional instruction.
Nakanishi (2015) conducted a meta-analysis of 34 studies and reported a mean effect size of d = 0.71 in favor of extensive reading over conventional methods.
This effect is likely attributable to the high volume of contextualized input that extensive reading provides, which allows learners to encounter target words repeatedly in meaningful contexts.
Orden: (b), (c), (a). «Se pierde la conexión hacia afuera del párrafo, no el contenido interpretativo.»

What do you lose?

When you remove the Link from the paragraph (PEEL → PIE), what is lost?

A. The evidence, because the Link contains the data.
B. The connection outward: the paragraph no longer ties back to the general argument, although the interpretation remains.
C. The main idea, because it moves to the end.
Discusión: ¿el párrafo sigue siendo convincente? En un párrafo de resultados (CER) la ausencia del Link es una convención del género.

Phrase bank: the PEEL family

Save the phrases you want to reuse. They go to your personal notebook.

One important factor in [topic] is... (PEEL/PIE) The main argument here is that... (MEAL) This paragraph examines the claim that... (TEEL)
According to [Author] (Year), ... [Author] (Year) found that...
This finding suggests that... (PEEL/TEEL) This evidence supports the argument because... (MEAL) An evaluation of these results shows that... (WEED)
Taken together, these findings indicate that... (PEEL/MEAL/TEEL)

Now you write

You will write your own paragraph using the PEEL family pattern. Allow about 15 minutes. Your writing is tracked for academic integrity: write it yourself, in your own words.

Choose a topic from your field and write the same paragraph twice in English. First follow PEEL (four moves). Then follow PIE (three moves, no Link). Label them “Version 1” and “Version 2”, and add one sentence saying which one you prefer and why.

Check your paragraph before submitting:
