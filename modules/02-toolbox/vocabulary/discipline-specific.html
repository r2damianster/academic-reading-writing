Discipline-Specific Vocabulary

Not All Academic Words Are Equal

When you read an academic text, three distinct vocabulary layers are operating at the same time. Recognising which layer a word belongs to tells you how to learn it and when to use it.

(1) General Academic Vocabulary (AWL)
Appears across all disciplines. Transferable. High frequency in academic texts.
analyse / framework / variable / assumption / demonstrate / constitute / assess / indicate
→ Learn these first. They work everywhere.
(2) Semi-technical Vocabulary
Common words with a discipline-specific meaning that differs from everyday use.
culture (sociology ≠ biology) / register (linguistics ≠ music) / capital (economics ≠ sociology) / depression (psychology ≠ geology)
→ These are the most dangerous — you recognise the word but may misread its meaning.
(3) Technical Vocabulary
Discipline-specific terminology with no common-language equivalent.
heteroscedasticity (statistics) / habitus (sociology) / apoptosis (biology) / episteme (philosophy)
→ Use only when necessary. Define on first use. Never use to impress.

Capas del Vocabulario:
• AWL: Palabras "comodín" que sirven para cualquier carrera (analizar, marco, variable).
• Semi-technical: Palabras comunes que cambian de significado en tu área (ej. "cultura" en biología vs. sociología).
• Technical: Jerga pura. Solo úsala si es necesario y defínela la primera vez.

How to Extract Vocabulary From a Text

Passive reading does not build active vocabulary. This four-step protocol turns any dense academic text into a vocabulary acquisition tool:

Step 1 — Identify the term
Mark every word you do not know or that seems to carry special weight in context.
Step 2 — Find the operational definition
Academic writers almost always define key terms within the first few uses. Look for "X, defined as..." / "X refers to..." / "by X, I mean..." — or for a contrasting term that implicitly defines by opposition.
Step 3 — Identify the core collocation
What verb or adjective most frequently accompanies this noun? What noun follows this verb? The collocation is more useful than the isolated word.
Step 4 — Find the contrasting term
Most academic concepts are defined in opposition to something: qualitative / quantitative, macro / micro, synchronic / diachronic. Knowing the opposite anchors the meaning.

General to Technical

These four sentences define related concepts at different levels of technicality. Put them in order from most general (accessible to any educated reader) to most technical (requires disciplinary knowledge).

Society is shaped by the unequal distribution of resources between individuals and groups.
Social stratification refers to the hierarchical arrangement of individuals and groups within a society based on their access to valued resources.
In Weberian terms, stratification is determined by the intersection of class (economic position), status (social honour), and party (political power).
Bourdieu reconceptualises stratification through the notion of field-specific capital, arguing that position within a field is determined by the volume and composition of economic, cultural, and social capital an agent possesses.
Orden:
1. Concepto general.
2. Definición académica estándar.
3. Aplicación teórica específica (Weber).
4. Jerga técnica avanzada (Bourdieu).

Technical or Accessible?

In each case, choose the option that uses vocabulary appropriate to the register and audience of the text described.

Context: An abstract for a specialist sociology journal.

This study applies Bourdieu's concept of habitus to examine how working-class students navigate the implicit cultural norms of elite university environments. This study looks at how poor students deal with the unwritten rules of fancy universities.

Context: A policy brief for a government ministry with non-specialist readers.

The heteroscedasticity of the residuals in the regression model necessitates the application of robust standard errors. The statistical analysis was adjusted to account for variability in the data that could otherwise affect the reliability of the results.

Context: A social science dissertation introduction defining a key concept.

Intersectionality is when different types of discrimination overlap. Intersectionality, as theorised by Crenshaw (1989), refers to the ways in which multiple systems of oppression — including race, gender, and class — intersect to produce distinct and compounding forms of disadvantage.

Respuestas: En el par 1, la primera opción es la correcta (audiencia especialista). En los pares 2 y 3, la segunda opción es la correcta (audiencia no especialista o definición apropiada al lector).
Tip: No uses jerga técnica (como "heteroscedasticity") cuando hables con gente de fuera de tu área (ej. un ministro o el público general). Tradúcelo a conceptos que puedan entender sin perder precisión.

Writing Task Instructions

You will write a paragraph introducing a key concept from your field of study. You must demonstrate awareness of all three vocabulary layers: use AWL vocabulary throughout, deploy at least one semi-technical term with its operational definition, and use at least one technical term appropriately introduced. Target length: 120–150 words. Allow approximately 12 minutes. Your writing will be tracked for academic integrity purposes.

Write a paragraph (120–150 words) introducing a key concept from your own discipline or area of study.

Your paragraph must demonstrate all three vocabulary layers:
— Use AWL vocabulary throughout as the backbone of your academic register (framework / analyse / constitute / indicate / demonstrate etc.)
— Define at least one semi-technical term operationally — explain how it is used in your discipline, not just its everyday meaning
— Introduce at least one technical term correctly — define it on first use, either with a relative clause (X, which refers to...) or an appositive (X — defined as...)
