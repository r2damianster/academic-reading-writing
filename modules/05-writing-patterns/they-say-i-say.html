They Say / I Say: Entering the Academic Conversation

Until now each pattern organised ideas inside a paragraph. This one organises the relationship between your voice and the voices of other researchers: first you say what others say (They Say), then what you say (I Say). A student who gives only their opinion writes in a vacuum; one who only summarises sources writes a report, not an argument.

Pattern: They Say / I Say · About 25 minutes · Book chapter 9

Based on: Rodríguez Zambrano, A. D. (2026). Academic writing patterns: A practical guide for EFL university students [Unpublished manuscript]. Universidad Laica Eloy Alfaro de Manta.

The model

In this open-access article on corrective feedback, the author does not defend a position of his own: he narrates a real debate between two researchers and closes it with his own judgement. Notice the reporting verbs.

Truscott, reflecting the views of teachers who adhere to process theories of writing, advanced the strong claim that correcting learners’ errors in a written composition may enable them to eliminate the errors in a subsequent draft but has no effect on grammatical accuracy in a new piece of writing (i.e., it does not result in acquisition). Ferris disputed this claim, arguing that it was not possible to dismiss correction in general as it depended on the quality of the correction—in other words, if the correction was clear and consistent it would work for acquisition. Truscott replied by claiming that Ferris failed to cite any evidence in support of her contention. To correct or not to correct written errors, then, remains contentious…
Ellis, R. (2009). Corrective feedback and teacher development. L2 Journal, 1(1), 3–18. https://doi.org/10.5070/l2.v1i1.9054

The pivot

Which pivot is rhetorically stronger when you partly disagree with a source?

“However, …”
“While [Author]’s point is well taken, …”
“Nevertheless, …”
Respuesta: B. «Un “however” es directo; un “while X is correct” reconoce mérito antes de disentir, y por eso es más fuerte retóricamente.» El Cap. 10 desarrolla esta idea (movida rogeriana).

Rebuild it: write the I Say

They Say: “Several scholars have argued that extensive reading alone is sufficient for second language vocabulary acquisition and that explicit vocabulary instruction is unnecessary in programs where students read at least 200,000 words per semester (Krashen, 2004; Nation, 2015).”

Write an I Say that responds with one of three options: agreement, disagreement or partial agreement. Include at least one datum or reference that supports your position.

The I Say takes a clear position (agreement, disagreement or partial agreement) and signals the pivot (e.g. “While…”, “However…”, “I agree with… and would add…”).
The position is backed by at least one datum, example or reference (not just an opinion) and the tone is academic, not personal.
Modelo (Apéndice C): «While extensive reading undoubtedly builds vocabulary through repeated exposure, research on incidental acquisition suggests that low-frequency or technical vocabulary rarely receives enough exposure through reading alone, which means some explicit instruction remains necessary for specialized registers.»

Spot it in the wild

Find a paragraph from the discussion of an open-access article in your field where the author cites another researcher before presenting their own interpretation.

  1. Where does the They Say end and the I Say begin? Which word or phrase marks the turn?
  2. Which reporting verb introduces the other position? Does it signal agreement, disagreement or neutrality?
  3. Is the author’s I Say based on their own evidence, or is it just an opinion? Which is more convincing?
  4. Does the author reject the other position completely, or propose a synthesis? What effect does that have on credibility?

Not graded. Bring your answers to class and compare them with a classmate.

Actividad no calificada. Compararla con un compañero.

Phrase bank: They Say / I Say

Save the phrases you want to reuse. They go to your personal notebook.

However, … / Nevertheless, … / Yet … While [Author]’s point is well taken, … / Although X is correct, …
I agree with [Author] that…, and would add that… By contrast, [Author] (Year) found that… / The evidence suggests otherwise. A more nuanced view would consider both… / The question is not whether X but how… In the present study, … / Data from [source] indicate that…

Now you write

You will write your own paragraph using the They Say / I Say pattern. Allow about 15 minutes. Your writing is tracked for academic integrity: write it yourself, in your own words.

Choose a source from your field with which you partly disagree. Write in English a paragraph of five to seven sentences that follows the They Say / I Say pattern: a They Say with a reporting verb, a clear pivot and an I Say with evidence.

Self-assessment rubric: They Say / I Say

Before you submit, check your paragraph against each row. You can ask the AI for a quick check (maximum two times): it suggests the marks and you decide. If three or more rows need work, rewrite before you hand it in.

CriterionAchievedNeeds work
This pattern
My They Say summarises the other position with an appropriate reporting verb, without distorting it.
The pivot between They Say and I Say is visible (however, yet, while, by contrast).
My I Say is not just an opinion: it includes at least one datum, example or reference.
The tone of my disagreement is academic, not personal or dismissive.
A reader who does not know the sources can follow the argument without getting lost.
General writing criteria
Academic register: formal tone, no contractions or colloquial expressions.
Vocabulary: precise, specific words instead of vague ones (thing, stuff, a lot of).
Cohesion: connectors and reference words link each sentence to the previous one.
Grammar and mechanics: complete sentences, agreement, punctuation, spelling.
Focus: the whole paragraph develops one main idea.
Certainty: hedges and boosters match the strength of the evidence.
