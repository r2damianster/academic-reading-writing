Midterm: Writing Patterns 2–7

This assessment has <strong>12 short writing tasks</strong>: six open-topic tasks and six paraphrase tasks, one pair for each pattern from lessons 2 to 7. Each task states the criteria it will be graded on. Write every answer yourself, in your own words.

Pattern: Patterns from chapters 2 to 7 · About 60 minutes · Book chapter 2–7

  1. The model — read a real, published passage
  2. Taking it apart — label each move
  3. Why this works — see the semantic wave
  4. Spot the pattern — recognise it in a second text
  5. Rebuild it — order, complete or repair a text
  6. Now you write — your own paragraph, with a checklist
  7. Phrase bank — keep the phrases you will reuse

Instructions

  • Part A: open-topic tasks. You choose the content; the pattern is assigned.
  • Part B: paraphrase tasks. You turn an informal remark into academic prose using the assigned pattern.
  • Each answer is graded on three criteria shown under the task.
  • Write complete sentences. Do not label the moves in the text unless the task asks you to name a variant.

Open topic 1: Academic sentence (given–new and light nominalization)

Topic: the use of technology in Ecuadorian classrooms.

Write <strong>two consecutive sentences</strong> about the use of technology in Ecuadorian classrooms, so that the second sentence picks up as given information something the first sentence presented as new, and includes at least one light nominalization.

Thematic continuity: the second sentence starts from something new in the first.
Correct use of at least one nominalization (e.g. “This expansion…”, “This integration…”).
Precision of academic vocabulary.
