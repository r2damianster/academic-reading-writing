What Is a Writing Pattern?

Academic writing is far more predictable than it looks. Behind the variety of articles, essays and reports there is a small repertoire of structures that repeat. In this lesson you learn what a pattern and a move are, and how this course will use them.

Patterns are functional skeletons

A pattern is a functional skeleton that repeats because it answers what the academic reader expects to find. Each of its components is called a move, a term coined by John Swales for every rhetorical step that performs a recognisable function inside a text.

The idea is very old: Aristotle defined rhetoric as the ability to see, in each case, what is available to persuade. Every pattern in this course is a modern answer to that ancient problem: how to arrange words so they do their job for the reader.

A pattern is not a straitjacket or a fill-in-the-blanks recipe. It is a learning scaffold. Expert writers master patterns so well that they can break them on purpose — and the last lesson of this category is about exactly that.

Aristóteles (trad. 1990) y Swales (1990) como marco. Insistir en «andamios, no jaulas» (Wesley, 2000 se discute en la última lección).

The wave: up and down between abstract and concrete

One idea will accompany you throughout: good academic paragraphs move up and down between the abstract and the concrete, like a wave. They present a general idea, descend into data and examples, and climb back to say what it all means.

When a paragraph does not work, it is almost always stuck up (pure abstraction) or down (data with no interpretation).

What patterns are for

Your paragraph does not convince the reader. According to this lesson, how can a pattern help you?

It writes the paragraph for you if you fill in the blanks.
It lets you compare your paragraph with the pattern and discover which move is missing or out of place.
It guarantees that the paragraph will receive a high grade.
Tres funciones (libro): reducen la carga cognitiva, alinean el texto con las expectativas del lector académico, y sirven de instrumento de diagnóstico.

How every lesson works

From the next lesson on, each pattern is taught in the same seven steps:

  1. The model — a real, published passage with its full reference. We never work with invented examples.
  2. Taking it apart — you label the passage move by move.
  3. Why this works — a short explanation, often with the wave.
  4. Spot the pattern — you recognise the pattern in a second text.
  5. Rebuild it — you order, complete or repair a text.
  6. Now you write — your own paragraph, with a checklist.
  7. Phrase bank — template phrases to save in your personal notebook.

Keep a notebook of phrases: every time a phrase bank is useful, save it and extend it with examples from your own field.

Now you write

You will write your own paragraph using the Writing patterns pattern. Allow about 8 minutes. Your writing is tracked for academic integrity: write it yourself, in your own words.

In three to five sentences, describe in English a text from your field that you have read recently (an article, a textbook chapter, a report). What structure did it repeat? For example: how did its paragraphs usually begin and end?
