CARS: Introductions That Create a Research Space

General → specific organises a sentence; CARS applies the same logic to a whole article introduction. John Swales identified three moves that almost every academic introduction performs, in order: establish a territory, establish a niche and occupy the niche.

Pattern: CARS (Swales) · About 20 minutes · Book chapter 13

The model

This is the complete introduction of the bilingual-advantage article you met in the Toulmin lesson, read now at introduction scale.

Bilingual advantages in learning novel languages have been attributed to both (i) direct transfer of previous language knowledge and skills and (ii) indirect effects of bilingualism, such as enhanced cognitive and metalinguistic skills (Hirosh and Degani, 2018).
Even though the literature on bilingual FL learning advantages is growing rapidly, evidence so far is limited to pupils with typical language development and it is unclear whether such advantages also extend to bilingual children with learning disabilities.
This paper aims to address this gap by comparing English as a foreign language (EFL) skills of monolingual and bilingual children with developmental language disorder (DLD) attending special education schools in the Netherlands.
Tribushinina, E., & Mackaaij, M. (2023). Bilingual advantages in foreign language learning: Evidence from primary-school pupils with developmental language disorder. Frontiers in Education, 8, Article 1264120. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1264120

Rebuild it: restore the three moves

These three sentences belong to the introduction of an article on subtitles and language learning. Put them in CARS order.

Subtitled video has been widely studied as a tool for incidental vocabulary acquisition in second language learners.
However, most studies have examined subtitles in fiction and entertainment content, and it remains unclear whether the same benefits apply to academic or documentary material.
The present study addresses this gap by comparing vocabulary gains among learners who watched subtitled academic documentaries with those who watched the same content without subtitles.
Orden (Apéndice C): (b), (a), (c): territorio sobre subtítulos y vocabulario → nicho (falta de estudios con material académico) → ocupación (estudio presente).

Spot it in the wild

Find the introduction of an empirical article from your field in open access. Read the first full paragraph and identify the three moves.

  1. Which sentence establishes the territory, with general claims or citations to previous studies?
  2. Which sentence opens the niche? Look for connectors such as however, yet, but, or words such as unclear, limited, remains.
  3. Which sentence occupies the niche? It often starts with “this study”, “this paper” or “we aim to”.

Not graded. Bring your answers to class and compare them with a classmate.
