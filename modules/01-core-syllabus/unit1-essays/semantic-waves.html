Semantic Waves

Pay attention to your instructor's explanation of semantic gravity, semantic density, and the concept of semantic waves. We will use these frameworks to analyze the academic article by Karl Maton (2020).

According to Maton (2020, p. 63), which of the following examples has the strongest semantic gravity (SG+)?

A. The name of a specific plant species in a Biology field report.
B. The process of photosynthesis as a general biological phenomenon.
C. A theory of historical causation applied across multiple periods.
D. The concept of 'cumulative knowledge-building' in education research.
Respuesta: A — Name of a specific plant species.
Referencia: Maton (2020), p. 63: "SG+ (stronger semantic gravity) where meaning is more dependent on its context... such as a specific event or object."
Tip: Ayudar a los estudiantes a ver que entre más específico y "local" sea el objeto, más gravedad semántica tiene.

Worldly codes (SG+, SD+)

Context-dependent and simpler — legitimacy accrues to meanings tied to specific, local situations.

Context-dependent but complex — characteristic of professional and vocational knowledge.
Clave:
• Rhizomatic: Abstracto y complejo (SG-, SD+).
• Prosaic: Concreto y simple (SG+, SD-).
• Rarefied: Abstracto pero vacío/jergas (SG-, SD-).
• Worldly: Concreto pero complejo (SG+, SD+).

Complete the following statements about semantic profiles and student achievement:

The high-achieving essay is characterized by a series of ___ that weave together different forms of knowledge, while the low-achieving essay traces a relatively low ___ (p. 69).

When a teacher translates a technical term into simpler, everyday language, this process is called (un__) ___, which weakens semantic density and strengthens semantic gravity (p. 73).

Respuestas: semantic waves, semantic flatline, unpacking, repacking.

In the Biology classroom example (pp. 72–74), when the teacher describes 'cilia' as 'the little hairs' and connects them to the effects of smoking, what semantic movement does this represent?

A. An upshift — the knowledge moves toward greater abstraction and complexity.
B. A downshift — the knowledge moves toward more context-dependent and simpler meanings (SG+, SD−).
C. A flatline — the semantic gravity and density remain constant throughout.
D. A repacking move — the teacher is condensing multiple meanings into a single technical term.
Respuesta: B — A downshift (SG+, SD-).
